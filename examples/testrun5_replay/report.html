The retention score is average retained performance across the full workload range. A score of 90% means this metric retained 90% of its reference performance on average across that range. Each metric also has an aggregate value in its original units.

Scores use the area under each metric’s retention curve divided by the workload span, with straight lines between adjacent measured levels. Actual workload spacing determines the weights. Compare scores over the same range, coordinate and reference. Values above 100% remain possible.

Reading the curves. Retention is P/P0 for FPS and T0/T for frame time. Degradation is 100(1 − retention); negative degradation means improvement. Local degradation rate is the change in degradation divided by the actual load interval. Its points sit at interval midpoints. A slope near zero can occur at either high or low absolute performance.

Provisional measurements are present. Hollow points have missing workload or capture metadata (including missing reference metadata). These curves describe the supplied captures at intended load coordinates; they do not verify true workload integrity or complete captures. Inspect the validity table and lap audit before comparing hardware.

Native per-lap measurements are summarized as equal-lap arithmetic means. Ratios and slopes are calculated afterward. Whiskers in this section are approximate 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals, not frame-level sampling errors. One run resamples whole sweeps; multiple runs resample whole runs. Fewer than 3 independent units: no inferred interval. 3–9 units: exploratory interval. Identical cluster support is required across dependent loads. The reference retention of 100% is an algebraic identity, not a claim of zero measurement uncertainty.

Definitions & limits

Reciprocal retention for lower-is-better metrics expresses throughput-equivalent performance loss. A latency increase of 100% corresponds to 50% retention, not zero retention. It is not the same as percentage latency increase. Negative degradation and retention above 100% are preserved.

Aggregate scores use uniform weighting in the native workload coordinate and trapezoidal integration. Different metrics and sweep modes are reported separately. Slopes depend on native workload units, baseline, and interval size; they are observed secants, not a continuous derivative. Small adjacent differences can be noisy. Confidence intervals do not model systematic drift or workload-count error.

Hardware/configuration labels are supplied by the user. Endpoint chicken counts are consistency checks, not verification of every moment of the capture. The original report section retains its historical calculations; this section can exclude additional laps when metric sample support or metadata contradicts the workload.
